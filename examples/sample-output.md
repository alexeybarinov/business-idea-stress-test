# Illustrative final output: what the result looks like

> **Fictional teaching example ONLY.** All customer, product, cost and pricing assumptions below are invented **for illustration**, not market facts, interviews, real prices or actionable forecasts. No outside market research has been performed here.

**Idea:** Subscription specialty-coffee delivery in one hypothetical city, aimed at remote workers

## 1. Initial evidence ledger

| Tag | Material assertion | Actual status | What would resolve it |
|---|---|---|---|
| F (founder input) | The founder wants to deliver within one city | Recorded from hypothetical founder | Confirm exact service radius |
| H | Remote workers find scheduled delivery important enough to pay extra | Untested | Conduct properly consented buyer interviews and test paid orders |
| U | Number of eligible buyers in the target delivery area | Unknown | Use a documented, location-specific source or measured sign-ups |
| U | Local alternatives and current delivered prices | Unverified | Inspect real local offers with dates |
| H | A reliable supplier can fulfill the first pilot | Untested | Obtain dated written quotes and minimum-order terms |

## 2. Core business risk

The convenience hypothesis has not been demonstrated. Even if remote workers consume coffee regularly, that fact alone would not establish willingness to pay for a subscription or to switch from existing purchasing routines. A small, ethical purchase test is more informative than a speculative total-addressable-market figure at this stage.

## 3. Illustrative unit economics

**The following numbers are hypothetical scenario inputs in an arbitrary monetary unit; none is a researched local price:**

| Input | Hypothetical value |
|---|---:|
| Revenue per order | 30 units |
| Variable fulfillment costs per order, including packaging | 18 units |
| Fixed cost per month | 1200 units |
| Refund/failed-delivery allowance | **Unknown: excluded, not zero** |
| Customer acquisition cost | **Unknown: no observed campaigns** |

- **Scenario contribution before omitted items:** `30 - 18 = 12 units per order`
- **Illustrative fixed-cost break-even ignoring omitted items:** `1200 / 12 = 100 orders per month`
- Because refunds, failed deliveries, taxes, founder time and acquisition are **not measured**, 100 orders is **not** a credible real-world break-even forecast
- A usable model would obtain verified supplier quotes and realistic returns/delivery costs before any investment decision

## 4. The strongest counterargument

People may already buy coffee conveniently from supermarkets or delivery apps without accepting subscription friction. Fulfillment failures could erase the thin illustrative contribution margin. The project should not proceed to a large launch based on expressed interest alone.

## 5. Conditional next step

**Classification for this fictional exercise: insufficient evidence**

Propose a short, transparently priced pilot with genuine purchase intent, privacy-respecting interviews and a founder-approved budget limit. Decide **before** launching what number of verified paid commitments and contribution per order would justify a larger experiment. If the founder cannot obtain a supplier agreement, test that dependency first.

**Important:** a real analysis would include current, cited market and competitor evidence if the host actually has access to research tools. This sample intentionally includes *no fabricated citations*.
