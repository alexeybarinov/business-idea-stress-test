# Example session: founder interview and early blocker check

This is a **fictional teaching example**, not real market research, a genuine customer interview, or a financial forecast.

**Founder:** “I want to launch a subscription service that delivers specialty coffee to remote workers. Grill the idea.”

**Assistant (Stage 1):** “Before we discuss marketing or supply costs: where would deliveries operate, and who exactly would pay—the individual remote worker or their employer?”

**Founder:** “Only my city; individuals pay. I have no supplier agreement and can invest up to 1500 in my local currency.”

**Assistant:** “What do you know about what this audience already pays for coffee, and what happens when they run out? Firsthand observations are fine; I will distinguish them from independently researched evidence.”

**Founder:** “I do not know. I just think subscriptions are convenient.”

**Assistant (checkpoint):**

- Confirmed founder inputs: one-city delivery, individual payer, available test budget stated by the founder, no supplier agreement
- Untested hypotheses: recurring delivery solves a serious problem; buyers value scheduling enough to pay a premium; supplier reliability can be arranged
- Next highest-impact question: “Can you fulfill even a small trial safely and legally without a supply agreement, or must a supplier first be secured?”

**Why this interaction matters:** The skill does *not* invent a local market size, label the idea promising, or create fabricated survey results. It resolves the operational and buyer-value assumptions before modeling revenue.
