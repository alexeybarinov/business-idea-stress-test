# Stage 3C — Competitor profiling

**Methodological inspiration:** Corey Haines' Competitor Profiling. Investigate **real** alternatives available to buyers in the specific target market. Do not stop at direct lookalike companies: include indirect substitutes, marketplaces, manual work, and the choice to continue as before.

## Procedure

1. Find verifiable direct competitors and 1–3 meaningful indirect substitutes. On small markets, a short authentic list is better than a fabricated comprehensive one
2. For each alternative capture: official link, operational geography, target segment, offer, published price and **date/currency**, acquisition/distribution channels, key distinctions, independently available reviews, and evidence gaps
3. Label vendor marketing claims as **vendor claims** unless third-party evidence independently corroborates them
4. When supported, check purchase conditions, subscription terms, transaction fees, minimum order size, warranties, service constraints, cancellation terms, and hidden-cost exposure
5. Compare **equivalent price units**, such as price per user-month, qualified lead, delivered order, or billable hour. Do not mix tax-inclusive and tax-exclusive or currencies without explicit notation
6. Identify competitor advantages and actual switching barriers. A missing feature is not a sustainable differentiator until customer demand for that feature is supported
7. Examine incumbent response risk: pricing power, network effects, locked-up channels, imitation, and reputation, distinguishing analysis from documented facts

### Deliverables

A dated competitor map, a defensible differentiation *hypothesis*, buyer switching barriers, threats from established providers, and unverified competitor-related claims.
