# Stage 3B — Customer research

**Methodological inspiration:** Corey Haines' Customer Research. Early customer personas are **hypotheses**, not verified individuals. Look for real, attributable customer language, public reviews, complaints, discussion threads, existing studies, and observable purchase behavior. Identify channel and demographic bias in every evidence source.

## Minimum research requirements

- Distinguish customer, end user, purchaser, and budget owner; define 2–4 plausible and genuinely reachable segments
- Identify the job to be done: situation, trigger, current workaround, desired result, fears, friction, trust concerns, and switching cost
- For each segment, examine external evidence of the problem, signs of money/time already being spent, and practical channels for reaching that segment
- Keep public self-reports distinct from representative research and new primary research. Reddit threads and forum posts are not randomized survey samples
- If willingness-to-pay evidence is missing, propose **behavioral tests**: an appropriately compliant paid reservation, pre-order, real buyer conversation about prior spend, or qualified inquiry with an explicit price. Choose based on jurisdiction and business model

## Real-customer interview mini-protocol

The founder must conduct or authorize any real interviews. Prompt **past behavior**, not hypothetical compliments:

- “Tell me about the last time this happened”
- “What did you do next?”
- “What did the solution cost, and who approved the spend?”
- “Which alternative did you consider, and what ruled it out?”

Avoid leading questions and pitching before understanding the problem. “Sounds interesting” is **not** validated purchase intent. Synthetic interview transcripts and fabricated buyer quotations are forbidden.

### Deliverables

A segment comparison, evidence-backed pain map with limitations, a neutral real-customer interview guide, and 1–3 feasible behavioral tests.
