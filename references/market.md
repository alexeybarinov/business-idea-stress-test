# Stage 3A — Market research

**Methodological inspiration:** xcrrr's Market Researcher. Scope the research to the actual operating **location, customer population, and time period**. If live research is available, prefer official statistics, industry associations, regulators, and independently checkable commercial data; record conflicting estimates rather than blending them silently.

## Research method

1. Define the product category, viable segments, languages, geographical reach, and boundaries. Distinguish B2B from B2C and user from purchaser; account for seasonality
2. Seek evidence of actual spending and observable substitutes. Search-result counts, founder intuition, and lively discussions **do not equal paying demand**
3. When inputs justify it, calculate: **TAM** = total eligible buyers × appropriate annual category spend; **SAM** = part of TAM serviceable given geography, model, and channels; **SOM** = the realistically obtainable portion of SAM subject to capacity and acquisition constraints. State unit, period, assumptions, and range
4. Triangulate top-down sources (published datasets and reports) with bottom-up inputs (reachable customers × credible purchase frequency × price). Explain discrepancies without unexamined averaging
5. Examine seasonal and structural demand patterns, dated trends, entry barriers, channel accessibility, platform constraints, local conditions, and data collection biases
6. Avoid presenting the global category as the addressable opportunity for a local or narrowly scoped business. If data are too thin, say **market size unverified** and identify exactly which parameters would resolve this
