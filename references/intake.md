# Stage 1 — Founder interrogation

**Methodological inspiration:** Matt Pocock's Grilling. Interview for clarity, not agreement. Never open with a giant questionnaire: first ask the founder to explain the business idea in their own words, then construct a dependency tree and ask the most consequential unanswered question. Skip anything already established. Adapt the language of questions to the founder's chosen language.

## Idea brief: fields to establish

- One-sentence offer: product/service and customer problem
- Operating location, language, digital/physical delivery, supply constraints
- Buyer, end user, and actual decision-maker when those are different people
- Current alternative, switching trigger, problem frequency, intensity of pain
- How and for what buyers would pay: transaction unit, price, fees, buying cycle
- Acquisition route and any defensible audience access or other advantage
- Business format: manufacturing, professional services, SaaS, marketplace/aggregator, resale, and so on
- Resources: starting capital, acceptable loss, time budget, relevant expertise, team and partners
- Operations: who fulfills the service, owns customer obligations, manages providers and platform dependencies
- Jurisdiction, permits, licensing, personal data, and contractual restrictions
- Founder objective and timeframe: side income, demand test, sustainable cash flow, eventual sale, etc.

## Question dependency tree

**A — Problem and buyer (first):** Who feels this pain? How is it solved today? What is wrong with the current solution? Have buyers paid for something similar? Who owns the budget?

**B — Offer and demand:** Why would buyers switch? How would they discover the offer? What are the geography and seasonality? What counts as competition? What actual market access or relevant prior experience is confirmed?

**C — Economics and execution:** Setup cost, acceptable maximum loss, desired testing horizon, monetization, fulfillment capacity, critical partners/platforms, ability to deliver the promise, returns, guarantees, and contractual obligations

**D — Reversibility:** Can the core proposition be tested before hiring, leasing, custom software, or sizable advertising? What outcome would make this venture unacceptable to the founder even if customers exist?

Ask the next question that changes the largest number of downstream decisions. Treat “I don't know” as a valid answer: log it as an unresolved assumption and suggest how to establish it. Help clarify ambiguous replies with neutral examples; never lead the founder toward a preferred answer. Every 3–5 exchanges, give a **brief checkpoint**: what is known, what is unknown, and the next pivotal question.

### Deliverables

An idea brief (~10–15 concise lines), resolved/unresolved decision tree, required research inputs, and a shortlist of high-risk assumptions. Move forward when enough is known; continue interviewing only when a critical ambiguity would make later analysis misleading.
